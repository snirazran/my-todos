12.	Anti-abuse	—	earn-rate	integrity	
(draft,	needs	review)
The	exploit	is	simple:	create	junk	tasks,	tick	them	instantly,	print	flies.	It	costs	us	gift-box	value,	corrupts	the	season	economy,	and	—
because	friends	earn	1	fly	for	every	2	you	earn	—	
it	prints	flies	for	everyone	connected	to	the	abuser	too.
	Any	response	has	to
stop	the	spread,	not	just	the	source.

Daily	ceiling	from	tasks:	30	flies	free,	60	premium.
	With	checklists	at	maximum	budget	the	theoretical	ceiling	is	90	free	/	180
premium,	though	reaching	it	requires	every	one	of	30	tasks	to	be	a	7-step	checklist.
No	simulated	player	comes	close	to	this	cap.
	Free	average	completes	6	tasks	a	day,	free	hardcore	and	premium	hardcore	12.	The	cap	sits
2.5×	above	our	heaviest	realistic	player	—	a	ceiling	nobody	legitimate	ever	touches,	which	is	exactly	what	a	cap	should	be.	It	removes	the
payoff	from	spamming	without	any	detection	logic	at	all.	Build	this	first;	everything	below	is	only	for	players	who	keep	going	after	a	cap	has
already	stopped	paying	them.
Present	the	cap	as	the	frog	being	full,	never	as	the	app	cutting	you	off.
	A	task	completing	in	silence	is	the	worst	possible	moment	in	a
productivity	app	—	the	user	did	the	work	and	got	nothing.	Use	the	existing	food	meter:	
"Your	frog	is	stuffed!	Come	back	tomorrow."
	Keep
showing	streak,	quest	and	season	progress	ticking	up	on	tasks	31+	so	the	work	visibly	still	counts.
Why	a	checklist	is	a	bad	way	to	cheat:
	3	flies	costs	roughly	10	interactions	through	a	7-step	checklist,	versus	3	interactions	through	3
separate	tasks.	The	sub-linear	budget	means	step-spamming	is	always	the	slower	path.
Layer	2	—	Detection	signals
Raw	task	count	is	a	bad	signal	—	someone	clearing	a	week-old	backlog	can	legitimately	tick	30	in	an	afternoon.	What	actually	separates
a	human	from	a	spammer:
Detection	counts	tasks	only.	Checklist	steps	are	invisible	to	every	signal	below.
	This	is	safe	precisely	because	the	checklist	budget	is
capped	—	there	is	nothing	to	gain	from	step-spamming,	so	there	is	nothing	to	police.	It	also	has	to	work	this	way:	four	steps	all	named	"Drink
Water"	is	textbook	low-entropy	titling,	and	someone	ticking	a	15-step	packing	list	in	four	minutes	blows	straight	through	the	burst	rule.
Legitimate	checklists	look	exactly	like	abuse	if	you	measure	at	the	step	level.

Layer	4	—	Automatic	recovery
7	consecutive	clean	days	clears	the	flag	automatically.
	No	support	ticket,	no	appeal	form,	no	human	in	the	loop.	A	wrongly-
flagged	user	simply	keeps	using	the	app	normally	and	the	restriction	dissolves.	This	is	the	cheapest	possible	insurance	against	our	own
false	positives.
Considered	and	not	recommended:
	a	separate	"abuser	shop"	or	parallel	season	with	worse	rewards.	It	means	building	and	maintaining
duplicate	content	for	our	least	valuable	users,	and	if	anyone	discovers	it	the	story	becomes	"app	secretly	punishes	players."	Capping	the	earn
rate	gets	nearly	all	the	same	benefit	for	none	of	the	build	cost	or	reputational	risk.
Open	questions	before	build:
Friend	income	is	now	the	largest	uncapped	faucet.
	A	capped	friend	still	transmits	up	to	15	flies	to	you,	so	20	friends	is	300/day
passive	no	matter	how	well-behaved	everyone	is.	Either	cap	how	many	friends	generate	passive	income,	or	cap	the	daily	passive	total.
Does	the	daily	cap	reset	at	local	midnight	or	on	a	rolling	24h	window?	Rolling	is	harder	to	game	across	timezones.
Do	we	log	flagged	accounts	for	manual	review	in	month	1	to	sanity-check	the	false-positive	rate	before	letting	it	run	unsupervised?
Does	an	ad-double	apply	to	a	checklist's	whole	budget	or	only	one	marker?
